\begin{abstract}
We report the completed hierarchy of fluctuation tests for the
boundary-driven nonlinear Schr\"odinger chain at
$(T_L,T_R,\gamma)=(10,2,0.1)$.  Direct sampling at
$n=10,20,30,40$ resolves finite-time large-deviation scaling but loses the
negative entropy tail before the asymptotic symmetry is reached.  At $n=2$,
two independent finite-time total-entropy constructions pass detailed and
integral fluctuation-relation audits.  For the long-chain question, controlled
cloning estimates satisfy every prospectively specified support,
observation-time, population, timestep, and selection-interval gate.  The
resolved SCGF pairs are numerically consistent with
$\psi_n(k)=\psi_n(1-k)$ at all four tested finite lengths.  This is a scoped
numerical verification, not an all-tilt proof or an infinite-chain result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Questions and observables}

The mentor's direct-sampling question concerns both tails of a finite-time
current, their threshold dependence, and their evolution with the averaging
time.  The thermodynamic fluctuation theorem concerns entropy production.
These are related but not identical questions.  In particular, the action
current is not substituted for entropy production: the joint simulations
show that action and bath heat are not tightly coupled at long chain lengths.

The projection-free Cartesian dynamics use $c_j=x_j+i y_j$.  Bath heat is
accumulated from the bath increment, with heat positive into the system, and
the medium entropy is
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma^{\rm m}=-\frac{Q_L}{T_L}-\frac{Q_R}{T_R}.
\end{equation}
For the stationary long-time test we use the right-bath entropy-current gauge
$\Sigma_R=-(T_R^{-1}-T_L^{-1})Q_R=-0.4Q_R$.  Its SCGF should obey the
Gallavotti--Cohen relation
\begin{equation}
 \psi_n(k)=\psi_n(1-k).
 \label{eq:gc}
\end{equation}

\section{Direct two-tail experiment}

For each $n=10,20,30,40$, the production data contain 1,000,064
non-overlapping base blocks of length $20$, aggregated without overlap to
$t=20,40,\ldots,200$.  Every block stores both bath heats, the energy change,
medium entropy, action current, stream and block identifiers, and the
first-law residual.  An independent recomputation performed 75,617 checks
with zero errors.

At fixed threshold, every raw-count-qualified action-current tail fit obeys
$\log P_t\simeq b-tI(A)$ with $R^2\geq0.98$.  Thus the data give clear
finite-time large-deviation evidence.  The centre becomes more
Gaussian-looking with time, but the $t=20$ far tails are heavier than the
Gaussian fixed by the full-sample variance.  Direct medium-entropy and heat
symmetry slopes move toward, but do not reach, their asymptotic reference
before negative-tail counts disappear.  The direct conclusion is therefore
``not verified at accessible times,'' not a violation.

\section{Finite-time total entropy for the two-site chain}

The finite-time total entropy adds the stationary endpoint contribution,
$\Delta s_{\rm tot}=\Sigma^{\rm m}+\Delta s_{\rm sys}$.  The accepted
parametric NESS endpoint analysis uses 1,048,576 blocks per condition.  Its
detailed-FT slope is $0.994496\pm0.00738$; the driven integral-FT estimate is
$\log\langle e^{-\Delta s_{\rm tot}}\rangle=0.002175$ with 95\% interval
$[-0.000744,0.005251]$.  Equilibrium density validation, model sensitivity,
stationarity, support, and numerical-integrity gates all pass.

An independent discrete path-ratio calculation gives a Crooks slope
$0.993352\pm0.00243$.  Its forward and reverse integral relations pass, and
the kernel entropy approaches the physical heat entropy at first order in the
timestep.  The agreement of the endpoint and path-ratio constructions is a
strong finite-time small-chain control; it does not establish the long-chain
limit.

\section{Controlled long-chain SCGF test}

The rare-event study fixes four independent seeds for each tilt member and
requires support, full-window and late-half symmetry, observation-time
convergence, and the relevant population, timestep, and selection-interval
controls.  Phase II supplied the missing long-chain controls without changing
the frozen tilts or tolerances.  All 56 Phase-II summaries, timeseries, and
timing logs are complete and finite, with zero midpoint failures.

At $n=30$, increasing the population from $N_c=2048$ to $4096$ passes both
individual-member gates and the paired-residual gate.  At the accepted
$N_c=4096$ baseline, timestep halving changes the paired residual by
$-0.001902$, while reducing the selection interval changes it by $-0.003157$;
both changes pass the absolute and two-standard-error criteria.  All four
individual control comparisons also pass.

The normalized residual magnitudes are approximately 0.04\%, 4.0\%, 4.9\%,
and 12.9\% for $n=10,20,30,40$.  The $n=40$ absolute gate passes, but the
relative uncertainty is larger because the SCGF itself is small.  This is an
important effect-size caveat rather than evidence of a resolved violation.

\section{Interpretation and allowed claim}

Direct sampling and cloning answer complementary parts of the same problem.
The former establishes finite-time large-deviation structure but becomes
rare-tail limited.  The latter resolves a preselected asymptotic SCGF pair and
passes the required numerical controls.  Together they support the statement:

\begin{quote}
For the boundary-driven projection-free Cartesian NLS chain at
$(T_L,T_R,\gamma)=(10,2,0.1)$, controlled rare-event estimates are consistent
with Gallavotti--Cohen SCGF symmetry at all four tested finite chain lengths
over the resolved complementary tilt pair.
\end{quote}

The result does not prove exact equality, cover every $k\in[0,1]$, establish
uniform accuracy as $n\to\infty$, or identify action current with entropy
production.  A stronger numerical claim would require a separately frozen
grid of additional tilts; an infinite-chain claim would additionally require
a dedicated finite-size study of the symmetry residual.
