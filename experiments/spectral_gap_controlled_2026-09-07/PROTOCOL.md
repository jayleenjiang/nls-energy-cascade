# Frozen protocol: controlled-integrator remeasurement of the n=3 relaxation rates

This protocol is written before inspection of any production autocorrelation or
rate output.  Smoke tests may test file shape, determinism, finite values, and
the absence of projections, but they must not be used to alter the scientific
parameters or the analysis gates below.

## Scientific question

The earlier stationary-autocorrelation experiment used the inherited
single-precision action--angle Euler--Maruyama map with polynomial trigonometry
and a positive-action projection.  This experiment asks whether the resulting
observable-dependent relaxation rates persist under a controlled discretization
of the same continuous canonical-bath NLS dynamics.

## Controlled dynamics and integrator

The production source is derived from `flux/NLS_entropy_ft.cpp`, whose
Cartesian convention is

```
c_j = x_j + i y_j,                 I_j = |c_j|^2,
E = H/2,
dc_j = i grad_j(E) dt                                      (interior),
dc_r = (i-gamma) grad_r(E) dt + sqrt(2 gamma T_r) dW_r     (r=1,3).
```

Away from zero action this is the Cartesian form of the canonical action--angle
SDE used by the earlier calculation.  It avoids the square-root singularity at
`I=0`, so no positive-action projection or floor is present.

Each fixed step consists of an implicit-midpoint Hamiltonian update followed by
Euler--Maruyama updates of the two Cartesian baths.  Left/right bath order is
alternated each step.  State and arithmetic are IEEE double precision; Gaussian
generation and reduced-angle measurement use the standard library `sin`, `cos`,
`log`, `sqrt`, and `atan2`.  The source must report midpoint nonconvergence,
nonfinite states, the minimum sampled action, and zero-radius events.  Any
projection/floor count is identically zero because there is no such operation.

## Frozen production matrix

- `n=3`, `gamma=0.1`.
- Bath cases: driven `(T1,T3)=(2,8)` and equilibrium `(5,5)`.
- Timesteps: `dt=1e-3` and `dt=2.5e-4`.
- 64 independently seeded trajectories per case.
- Burn-in `500` and stationary measurement duration `1000` per trajectory.
- Snapshot interval `0.01`, including the snapshot at measurement time zero:
  100001 snapshots per trajectory.
- Base seeds:
  - driven, `dt=1e-3`: `2026090801`;
  - driven, `dt=2.5e-4`: `2026090802`;
  - equilibrium, `dt=1e-3`: `2026090803`;
  - equilibrium, `dt=2.5e-4`: `2026090804`.
- Stream seeds are the declared deterministic `stream_seed(base_seed,id)`
  values generated by the source.

The saved float32 observable columns (the simulated state remains double) are:

1. `cos_theta1`
2. `sin_theta1`
3. `cos_theta3`
4. `sin_theta3`
5. `cos_theta1_minus_theta3`
6. `I2`
7. `I1_plus_I3`
8. `I1_minus_I3`
9. `cos_even_sum = cos_theta1 + cos_theta3`
10. `cos_odd_diff = cos_theta1 - cos_theta3`
11. `sin_even_sum = sin_theta1 + sin_theta3`
12. `sin_odd_diff = sin_theta1 - sin_theta3`

At equilibrium, the labels even/odd refer exactly to endpoint exchange
`1 <-> 3`.  In the driven case the bath temperatures break that symmetry, so
the same combinations are retained as diagnostic coordinates, not exact
generator sectors.

## Frozen autocorrelation and plateau analysis

The analysis is the same rule as the previous study:

- pooled stationary mean across the 64 independent trajectories;
- unbiased FFT autocovariance through lag `10`;
- the same 122-point zero-plus-geometric lag grid;
- trajectory-level standard errors and 2000 trajectory bootstrap replicates;
- five-point log derivative of positive autocorrelation values;
- candidate support requires `t >= 1`, `C/SE >= 3`, `C>0`, and negative local
  rate;
- at least eight contiguous grid points spanning a factor `1.5` in time;
- maximum local-rate range no greater than 20% of the interval median magnitude;
- among eligible intervals, choose maximum log-time span, breaking ties toward
  the later interval;
- the accepted rate is a weighted straight-line slope of `log C(t)` over the
  frozen interval, with a trajectory-bootstrap 95% interval;
- stationarity/burn-in check: RMS difference between full-measurement and
  discard-first-quarter autocovariances over `t<=5`, divided by `|C(0)|`, must
  be at most 5%.

The previous pooled common-plateau rule is retained: at least three individually
resolved observables, overlapping plateau-time intervals, rate spread no more
than 20% of the median magnitude, and a nonempty intersection of their 95% CIs.
The report must state exactly which observables resolved and must not describe a
subset result as agreement of all requested observables.

For the equilibrium case, the same common-plateau calculation is also reported
separately for exact exchange-even and exchange-odd combinations.  Different
sector-leading rates are allowed by symmetry and do not by themselves imply a
numerical failure; the user's pooled interpretation gate is nevertheless also
reported verbatim.

## Frozen early oscillation analysis

For `I1_minus_I3`, `cos_odd_diff`, and `sin_odd_diff`, fit on the predeclared
window `[0.05,1.00]`

```
C(t)=exp(lambda_R t)[A cos(lambda_I t)+B sin(lambda_I t)].
```

Use trajectory-level bootstrap (2000 replicates), report `lambda_R`,
`lambda_I`, their 95% intervals, period, first zero, and Delta AIC relative to
the nested real exponential.  Oscillation is called resolved only if at least
95% of bootstrap fits succeed, the lower 95% limit of `lambda_I` is positive,
`Delta AIC <= -10`, and `lambda_I >= pi/(1.00-0.05)`.  No fit-window change is
allowed after seeing data.

## Frozen timestep and interpretation gates

For each observable resolved at both timesteps, call it timestep-consistent only
when the 95% rate intervals overlap and the point estimates differ by no more
than 20% of their median magnitude.  Unresolved observables remain unresolved;
they are not silently omitted from the narrative.

The final top-level classification is fixed in advance:

1. If the requested observables satisfy the frozen common-rate rule at both
   timesteps and the timestep-consistency gate, classify the old disagreement
   as a numerical artefact and quote the common rate.
2. If disagreement remains under the controlled integrator at both timesteps,
   classify the accessible-window disagreement as dynamical and the spectral
   gap as unresolved.
3. If agreement occurs at `2.5e-4` but not at `1e-3`, classify the earlier
   measurement as timestep-limited.

Because exact exchange symmetry can isolate sectors at equilibrium, the report
must additionally state sector-specific outcomes even if the pooled gate fails.

No parameter, fit window, trajectory length, plateau rule, or interpretation
gate may be changed after any production output exists.
