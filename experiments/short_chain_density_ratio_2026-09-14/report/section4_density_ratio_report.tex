\begin{abstract}
We tested a preregistered Gibbs-anchored representation of the five-dimensional
stationary density, using independent-trajectory train, validation and blind
test splits.  The exact equilibrium known-answer stage passed, but neither of
the two equilibrium-admissible model classes passed the driven validation
gates.  The driven blind split therefore remained sealed.  The result is a
methodological partial result, not a numerical solution of the NESS density.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen question and method}
The previous free normalized neural density did not reproduce the known Gibbs
stationary equation.  Here the equilibrium density is built in as a reference,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_\eta(X)=\rho_0(X)\frac{\exp f_\eta(X)}{Z_\eta},
 \qquad \rho_0(X)\propto \exp[-E(X)/5].
\end{equation}
The network therefore learns only the density ratio.  Inputs use bounded action
features $I_j/(I_j+s_j)$ and periodic Fourier features of the two bond angles.
The preregistered candidates were a linear model and two tanh MLPs with widths
$32\times32$ and $64\times64$, each with seeds 5201--5203.  All inputs are the
existing projection-free float64 trajectories; no new stochastic simulation
was performed.

Streams were split at trajectory level into 32 training, 16 validation and 16
blind-test trajectories.  For the equilibrium known-answer test, each split was
further divided deterministically into disjoint pseudo-target and reference
streams.  The exact normalized log density ratio is then zero.

\section{Equilibrium known-answer gate}
The linear and $64\times64$ candidates passed every validation gate for all
three seeds.  The $32\times32$ family was rejected because one seed had median
$|\Lag\rho/\rho|=0.1227$, above the frozen 0.10 bound.  After freezing the
admissible set, the equilibrium blind split was opened once.  All six retained
fits passed.  Blind centered log-ratio RMS was 0--0.00893, ROC AUC was
0.5000--0.5026, and reference-weight ESS fraction was 0.99992--1.00000.

This establishes that the Gibbs anchor and density-ratio implementation retain
the exact equilibrium law without manufacturing a spurious correction.  It
does not by itself establish that the correction family is expressive enough
for the driven state.

Importance support was not the limitation: every fit retained more than 95\%
of the nominal reference-sample ESS.  All frozen marginal-TV gates also passed;
the largest TV distance, for the joint two-angle histogram, was 0.0377--0.0420
against the 0.05 bound.  Across-seed log-ratio disagreement also passed.

\section{Verdict and claim boundary}
No model passed the driven validation gate, so the frozen selector recorded
that there was no admissible driven candidate.  In accordance with the protocol,
the driven blind split was not read and the coarse-timestep stage was not run.

The result is therefore \textbf{PARTIAL}: the Gibbs anchor fixes the previous
equilibrium support/normalization problem, but the tested density-ratio fits do
not solve the driven stationary Fokker--Planck equation.  Agreement of coarse
marginals cannot be promoted to a joint NESS-density claim, because the same
fits miss the stationary current observables by 15--30 standard errors.

The next density protocol should change the optimization and validation target
so that held-out stationarity and current-sensitive weak constraints influence
model selection.  The present thresholds must not be relaxed post hoc.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Reproducibility.}
All 256 trajectories match the frozen byte counts and SHA-256 manifest.  Exact
commands, seeds, source hashes, raw metrics and the I/O amendment accompany
this report.
